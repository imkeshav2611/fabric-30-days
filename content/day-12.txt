q: How do you handle deletes in your incremental loads?

caption:
The first version of most pipelines reloads everything. It works until the table grows and the nightly window does not.

An incremental load is the fix. Remember where the last load stopped, fetch only what changed, and merge it in. A MERGE statement updates the rows that exist and inserts the ones that do not, in a single transaction. In Fabric it works in Spark and is generally available in the Warehouse, and Copy job can do watermark-based incremental copy without custom code.

The idea is simple. The edge cases are where the work is. A watermark on a modified date never sees deletes. Rows that arrive late are skipped if the bookmark has already moved. A retry that inserts the same rows twice turns a fast load into a wrong one.

What makes this dangerous is that failures are quiet. The job succeeds, the counts look plausible, and the table drifts from the source a little each day. A scheduled reconcile against the source is cheap insurance.

On a Databricks platform I worked on for a financial services company, we processed over 10 million records a day through incremental pipelines. Full reloads were never an option at that volume, so getting these details right was the job.
