The problem

Most pipelines exist only to copy data.

2Step twoSchedule the copy

Two copies cost twice the storage, and they are never quite the same.

The simple version

A desktop shortcut to a file.

A shortcut on your desktop is not the file. It is a signpost. Double-click it and you open the real thing.

A OneLake shortcut does the same for a folder or table that lives somewhere else.

✓Looks likeA normal folder in the lake
✓Actually isA pointer to the source
✓Copies madeNone
How it works

For engineers

The parts that trip people up

Identity depends on the targetShortcuts inside OneLake check the calling user. External ones use the stored connection's credentials.
Deleting has two meaningsRemoving the shortcut leaves the source untouched. Deleting a file inside it deletes that file at the source.
Speed is the source's speedQueries still read from the other cloud, with its latency and egress cost. Caching is available for some sources.
Format still mattersA shortcut to CSV gives you files, not a table. Delta or Parquet is what makes it queryable.
The honest part

Shortcut or copy?

Shortcut

  • Data is already in Delta or Parquet
  • Another team owns and maintains it
  • You want zero duplication

Copy the data

  • The source is slow or far away
  • You need to reshape it heavily anyway
  • The source may change without warning

The catch: a shortcut makes you depend on someone else's storage. If they rename a folder or rotate a key, your reports break and nothing in your workspace changed.

Watch out

Four things to check first

1Who owns the connection an external shortcut relies on
2Egress charges when the source is in another cloud
3Access rules: who can now reach the data through you
4A plan for when the source moves or changes

A shortcut removes the copy, not the dependency.
