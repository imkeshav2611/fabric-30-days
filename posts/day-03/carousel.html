The simple version

Rooms in a building, one power meter.

A workspace is a room. A team keeps its lakehouses, pipelines and reports in it.

A capacity is the electricity. Every room plugged into it shares the same supply.

How the meter works

1Compute is counted in Capacity Units
2You buy a size: F2 up to F8192
3Pay as you go can be paused. Reserved costs less
4Bursting lets a job run faster than the size allows
5Smoothing spreads that usage over time
ThrottlingSustained overuse first delays interactive work, then rejects it, and finally rejects background jobs too.
For engineers

Details worth knowing early

Interactive and background are smoothed differentlyA report click is smoothed over minutes. A scheduled job is spread across 24 hours.
F64 is a licensing lineFrom F64 up, people with a free licence and the Viewer role can open Power BI content. Below it, viewers need Pro.
The Capacity Metrics app is the source of truthIt shows which item used how much, and when throttling started.
Overage is now an optionCapacity overage bills spikes at a premium rate instead of throttling. It is on by default for new F capacities.
The honest part

One capacity or several?

One shared capacity

  • Simple to run and to explain
  • Idle compute is shared, not wasted
  • Fine for a small or new team

Split capacities

  • Production reports need protection
  • Dev experiments are unpredictable
  • Departments want separate bills

The catch: a Spark experiment in a dev workspace and the CEO's dashboard can share one meter. Separate them before it hurts, not after.

Watch out

Four mistakes I would avoid

1Sizing from a guess. Start small, measure, then resize
2Dev and prod on one capacity with no guardrails
3Nobody owns the Metrics app, so throttling is a surprise
4Leaving pay-as-you-go running on nights and weekends

Capacity is a design decision, not a purchase order.
