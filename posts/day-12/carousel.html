Incremental loading.
Faster, cheaper, and easy to get wrong.

WatermarkMERGEChange data
The problem

Reloading everything, every night, stops scaling.

1Year oneThe full load takes 10 minutes
2Year threeIt takes four hours
3The data that changedLess than one percent

You are paying to move and rewrite data that is exactly the same as yesterday.

The simple version

Read today's newspaper, not the archive.

To catch up on the news, you do not reread every paper ever printed. You read what came out since you last looked.

An incremental load does that: remember where you stopped, fetch only what is new.

✓BookmarkThe last point you loaded

How it works

The three steps

1Find the changes: a modified date, an ID, or a change feed
2Bring only those rows into the lake
3Merge them: update what exists, insert what is new
4Move the bookmark only after the load succeeds
MERGE is the keyOne statement updates matching rows and inserts new ones, as a single transaction.
For engineers

Where it goes wrong

Deletes are invisibleA watermark on "modified date" never sees a deleted row. You need a change feed or a periodic compare.
Late dataRows that arrive after the bookmark has moved are skipped unless you reload a safety window.
Reruns must be safeIf a retry inserts the same rows twice, the load is not incremental, it is wrong.
Trust but verifyA scheduled full reconcile catches drift before the business does.
The honest part

Incremental or full load?

Incremental

  • Large tables with few daily changes
  • A reliable change marker exists
  • Loads must finish in a tight window

Full reload

  • Small tables that load in seconds
  • No trustworthy change marker
  • History gets rewritten often

The catch: an incremental load fails quietly. It still finishes, the row counts look fine, and the table drifts a little further from the source every day.
