The simple version

Raw vegetables, chopped, cooked.

A kitchen does not cook straight from the delivery crate. It stores, washes and chops first, then cooks to order.

Data works the same way, in three stages you can inspect separately.

✓BronzeExactly as it arrived
✓SilverCleaned and consistent
✓GoldShaped for a business question
How it works

What happens in each layer

1Bronze: land the data as-is and keep its history
2Silver: fix types, remove duplicates, apply rules
3Silver: join sources into shared, trusted tables
4Gold: aggregate and model for reports and KPIs
The pointWhen logic changes, you rebuild from Bronze. You do not go back and ask the source again.
For engineers

Decisions that shape the build

Layers are a contract, not a ruleThree is a convention. What matters is that each layer has a clear promise about quality.
Where layers liveMicrosoft's guidance is one lakehouse per layer, each in its own workspace, so access and ownership stay clean.
Gold can be a warehouseIf consumers think in SQL, serve Gold from a Warehouse and keep Bronze and Silver in a Lakehouse.
Materialized lake viewsDeclare Silver and Gold as Spark SQL. Fabric stores them as Delta, orders the refreshes and applies quality rules.
The honest part

Do you always need three layers?

Worth it when

  • Several sources feed the same reports
  • Logic changes often
  • You must show what arrived and when

Overkill when

  • One small, clean source
  • A short-lived prototype
  • Nobody will maintain three layers

The catch: medallion fails when Silver quietly becomes a second Bronze. If Silver has no quality rules, you have added a layer and gained nothing.
