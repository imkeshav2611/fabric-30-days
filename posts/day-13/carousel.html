The problem

Tables get slower without anyone changing them.

1Every loadAdds a few more small files
2Every updateLeaves old files behind
3After six monthsThousands of files per table

The query has to open every one of them. The data did not grow. The mess did.

The simple version

Tidying a messy cupboard.

Put things away in a hurry for months and the cupboard fills with half-empty boxes. Finding anything takes longer each week.

Maintenance is repacking into full boxes and throwing out what is no longer needed.

✓OPTIMIZEMerge small files into big ones
✓V-OrderArrange files for faster reading
✓VACUUMRemove files no longer in use
How it works

The three commands

1OPTIMIZE rewrites many small files as fewer large ones
2V-Order sorts and compresses Parquet for fast reads
3VACUUM deletes old files past the retention period
4The Maintenance dialog on a lakehouse table runs all three
Default retention is 7 daysVACUUM keeps a week of history. Shorter than that and time travel stops working for older versions.
For engineers

What changed, and what to check

V-Order is off by default in new workspacesThe default profile favours fast writes. Reports read faster with V-Order, so turn it on for serving tables.
Resource profiles set the defaultswriteHeavy is the default. The read-heavy profiles enable V-Order and optimised writes for you.
Auto compaction is opt-inMicrosoft recommends it for ingestion tables. It compacts after writes so you do not have to schedule it.
Order mattersCompact first, then vacuum. Vacuuming first just removes files you were about to rewrite.
The honest part

How often is enough?

Maintain often

  • Tables loaded many times a day
  • Tables that Power BI reads directly
  • Tables with frequent updates or deletes

Leave alone

  • Large tables that are rarely written
  • Bronze tables nobody queries
  • Tables rebuilt in full each run

The catch: maintenance uses capacity too. OPTIMIZE on a huge table at 9 a.m. competes with everyone's reports. Schedule it like any other heavy job.

From real work

Maintenance on a real platform

A Databricks platform for a financial services company, with daily loads into Delta Lake.

1Partitioning on the large Delta tables
2Compaction to merge small files into larger ones
3Z-Ordering to keep related rows close together
4Query scan times improved as a result

The same three ideas apply to every Delta table in Fabric.
