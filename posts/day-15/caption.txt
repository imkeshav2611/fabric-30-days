Microsoft Fabric, Day 15 of 30: mirroring.

Getting operational data into an analytics platform is usually the first pipeline a team builds, and one of the most fragile.

Mirroring removes that pipeline for supported sources. You point Fabric at a database and choose the tables. Fabric takes a snapshot, then keeps reading changes and writes them into OneLake as Delta tables. A SQL analytics endpoint sits on top, so the data is queryable without any further work.

The list of sources has grown a lot. It now covers Azure SQL, SQL Server, Cosmos DB, PostgreSQL, Snowflake, Oracle, SAP and Google BigQuery among others, plus open mirroring for systems that can push their own changes.

The pricing detail worth knowing: the replication compute is free, and storage is free up to 1 TB per capacity unit. Queries against the mirrored data use capacity like anything else.

What mirroring does not do is model the data. You get a faithful copy of an operational schema, with all its quirks. A Silver layer on top is still needed before anyone builds reports on it.

No project story today. Mirroring replaces ingestion code, not thinking.
