Microsoft Fabric, Day 4 of 30: the Lakehouse.

If a Fabric project has one item at its centre, it is usually a Lakehouse.

The idea is simple. A data lake accepts any file but is awkward to query. A warehouse is easy to query but strict about what goes in. A Lakehouse gives you both in one item: a Files area for raw data in any format, and a Tables area for cleaned data stored as Delta tables.

What makes it practical is the SQL analytics endpoint. Every Lakehouse gets one, so the tables Spark writes can be queried with T-SQL and used by Power BI without exporting anything.

The detail that surprises people: that endpoint is read-only. Writes come from Spark, or from ingestion tools such as pipelines and dataflows. For a team that thinks in SQL, that matters a lot, and it is the main reason the Lakehouse versus Warehouse question exists.

On a people-analytics platform I worked on, one Lakehouse held both the raw files and the Gold tables that the reports used. Fewer moving parts meant fewer places for numbers to drift.
